Status & Identity

Living Soul

A living man or woman, created by God, possessing unalienable rights by virtue of existence. Distinguished from legal fictions and artificial persons.

Artificial Person

A legal entity created by law, such as a corporation, trust, or government agency. Has no rights except those granted by its creator.

Citizen

A member of a political community who owes allegiance and is entitled to protection. In ecclesiastical context, refers to a naturalized member of the Ecclesia.

Naturalization

The process by which a living soul becomes a member of the Ecclesia, including declaration of status and acceptance of the covenant.

Sovereign

One who possesses supreme authority not derived from any external source. A living soul exercising their inherent divine authority.

Strawman

The legal fiction created by government at birth, represented by the ALL CAPS name. Distinct from the living soul.

Sui Juris

Latin: "Of one's own right." A person with full legal capacity and rights, not under guardianship or disability.

Types of Law

Divine Law

The eternal law of God as revealed through scripture and natural order. The highest form of law, superseding all human-made law.

Natural Law

Universal moral principles inherent to human nature, discoverable through reason. The basis of unalienable rights.

Common Law

Law derived from custom, usage, and judicial precedent rather than statutes. Based on reason and natural justice.

Ecclesiastical Law

The internal rules and governance of religious organizations. Protected under the First Amendment and 508(c)(1)(A).

Admiralty/Maritime Law

Law governing commerce on the seas and waterways. Often improperly applied on land through contractual consent.

Statutory Law

Written law enacted by legislatures. Applies only to those who consent or fall under statutory jurisdiction.

Canon Law

The body of laws and regulations developed by ecclesiastical authority for church governance.

Covenant Law

Sacred agreements between parties, binding in honor and conscience. The basis of trust relationships.

Commerce & UCC

Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)

A comprehensive set of laws governing commercial transactions in the United States. Provides the basis for secured transactions and commercial remedies.

UCC-1 Financing Statement

A legal form filed to perfect a security interest in collateral. Used to establish priority in commercial transactions.

Security Interest

A legal claim in property given by a debtor to a creditor to secure payment of a debt or performance of an obligation.

Commercial Lien

A claim against property to secure payment of a debt. Can be voluntary (consensual) or involuntary (statutory or common law).

Bill of Exchange

A written order directing one party to pay a certain sum to another party. A negotiable instrument under UCC Article 3.

Promissory Note

A written promise to pay a specified sum at a specified time. Creates a debt obligation.

Surety Bond

A guarantee of performance or payment. The surety agrees to be responsible if the principal fails to perform.

Setoff

The right to cancel mutual debts. When two parties owe each other, the amounts can be offset against each other.

Discharge

The release from an obligation, debt, or duty. In commerce, debts are discharged rather than paid.

Governance

508(c)(1)(A)

Section of the Internal Revenue Code providing automatic tax exemption for churches and ecclesiastical organizations without requiring IRS approval.

Ecclesiastical Organization

A religious body organized for worship, teaching, and carrying out religious purposes. Protected from state interference in internal matters.

Trust

A fiduciary relationship in which one party (trustee) holds property for the benefit of another (beneficiary).

Trustee

One who holds legal title to property for the benefit of another. Has fiduciary duties to the beneficiary.

Beneficiary

One who receives benefits from a trust or other arrangement. Has equitable interest in trust property.

Fiduciary

One who has a duty to act in good faith and in the best interest of another party.

Power of Attorney

A document authorizing one person to act on behalf of another. Can be general or limited in scope.

Ordination

The act of conferring ministerial authority. Creates ecclesiastical standing and authority to perform religious functions.

Property

Allodial Title

Absolute ownership of real property, free from any superior landlord or sovereign. The highest form of property ownership.

Land Patent

The original document by which the government conveyed title to land. Creates the highest form of title.

Fee Simple

An estate in land with unconditional ownership rights. The most complete form of ownership under statutory law.

Deed

A written instrument that conveys real property from one party to another. Various types include warranty, quitclaim, and trust deeds.

Encumbrance

A claim or liability attached to property, such as a lien, mortgage, or easement.

Chattel

Personal property, as distinguished from real property. Movable property.

Latin Legal Terms

Ad Hominem

"To the person." An argument attacking the person rather than their position.

Bona Fide

"In good faith." Done honestly and without fraud or deceit.

Caveat Emptor

"Let the buyer beware." The principle that the buyer is responsible for checking quality before purchase.

De Facto

"In fact." Existing in reality, whether or not legally recognized.

De Jure

"By law." Existing by right or legal establishment.

Ex Post Facto

"After the fact." A law that retroactively changes the legal consequences of actions.

Habeas Corpus

"You have the body." A writ requiring a person under arrest to be brought before a court.

In Personam

"Against the person." Legal action directed against a specific person.

In Rem

"Against the thing." Legal action directed against property rather than a person.

Nunc Pro Tunc

"Now for then." An action that has retroactive legal effect.

Pro Se

"For oneself." Representing oneself without an attorney.

Quid Pro Quo

"Something for something." An exchange of value, consideration in contracts.

Res Judicata

"A matter judged." A matter that has been finally decided by a court and cannot be relitigated.

Stare Decisis

"To stand by things decided." The doctrine of following precedent in legal decisions.

Sui Generis

"Of its own kind." Unique, in a class by itself.
